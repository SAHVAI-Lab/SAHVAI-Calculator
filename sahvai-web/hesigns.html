ICH expansion signs in the browser

Drop a head CT with an intracerebral hemorrhage to find the CT signs that predict hematoma expansion: blend, black hole, hypodensity, swirl, island and satellite signs, irregular shape, heterogeneous density and fluid level on non-contrast CT, and spot-sign candidates on CT angiography. It then gives the BAT and BRAIN scores and prepares the image patches of the DeepMedX end-to-end expansion pipeline. Everything runs on this device.

What you need

  • An axial head CT with a spontaneous ICH: non-contrast for the NCCT signs, CT angiography for spot-sign candidates. DICOM zip or folder, NIfTI or NRRD.
  • A recent desktop Chrome or Edge; WebGPU is fastest. About 30 s to 3 min per scan.

CT signs of hematoma expansion reference

The figure of Liu et al. (GeroScience 2026, Fig. 2) shows ten panels and nine signs (the blend sign twice): three contrast-extravasation signs (spot, iodine, leakage) and six NCCT signs (blend, black hole, island, satellite, hypodensity, swirl). Irregular shape, heterogeneous density and fluid level complete the NCCT set of the 2019 consensus (Morotti et al., Ann Neurol). Schematics drawn for this page (anterior at the top).

How the signs are read

  1. Hematoma. The largest connected IPH component of the SAHVAI segmentation; each axial section is hole-filled so encapsulated low-density areas stay inside it. Density signs are read on sections with at least 30% of the largest section's area (end sections are partial-volumed) and not on sections with dense metal (a coil or clip, ≥ 2000 HU) in the brain; a catheter near the clot is flagged.
  2. Low-density regions. The CT is lightly smoothed (σ ≈ 0.5 mm). Pixels ≥ 12 HU below the section's hyperdense reference (75th percentile of the clot core) and ≥ 12 HU are candidates; a region must reach ≥ 1.5 mm inside the clot edge, be ≥ 7 mm² and ≥ 2 mm wide. It is encapsulated if growing it 1.5 mm through low-density pixels does not reach the clot edge. Its HU difference is measured against the hyperdense ring 0.5–2 mm around it.
  3. Density signs. Hypodensity: encapsulated, ≥ 15 HU difference. Black hole: encapsulated, ≥ 28 HU. Swirl: encapsulated, iso- or hypodense to brain (≤ brain + 8 HU), on ≥ 2 contiguous slices. Blend: open to the edge, ≥ 18 HU, ≥ 15 mm², ≤ 60% of the section, a ≥ 5 mm interface with a sharp edge (≥ 4 HU/mm), and still denser than brain. Fluid level: the low-density part lies wholly above a straight (RMS ≤ 0.75 mm), near-horizontal (≤ 10°) interface ≥ 8 mm long spanning half the clot. Heterogeneous density: ≥ 3 low-density foci (≥ 2 mm²) on the largest section.
  4. Shape signs. Lobules: what an opening with a disk (radius 0.35 × the section's equivalent radius, 2–8 mm) removes from the section; bubble- or sprout-like ones are ≤ 80 mm² with a neck narrower than themselves. Separate small hematomas: other blood labels or compact (elongation ≤ 3) foci of 50–100 HU, ≥ 2 mm², ≥ 4 mm inside the brain, away from metal and calcification. Satellite: a separate focus < 10 mm across, 1–20 mm from the clot. Island: ≥ 3 separate, or ≥ 4 separate plus connected, on one section. Irregular shape: ≥ 2 lobules or separate foci within 10 mm on the largest section.
  5. Contrast. A scan with ≥ 2 mL of 130–600 HU voxels inside the brain is treated as contrast-enhanced: NCCT signs are not read, and foci ≥ 120 HU, ≥ 1.5 mm, inside the clot and not touching its edge are spot-sign candidates. Thin CTA slices are averaged into 5 mm slabs, which lowers small spots' HU.

Yale model. Their pipeline as published: brain window 0–80 HU, skull stripped, padded or cropped to 512 × 512 × 48 (slices resampled to 5 mm when there are more than 48), SegResNet by 256 × 256 × 32 sliding window; then mask and CT resampled to 1 mm, the slices with blood ± 16 mm, the CT within 40 mm of the clot as a second channel, resized and centre-cropped to 128 × 128 × 96, 3D DenseNet121 with Monte-Carlo dropout. Checked against the authors' code in Python: same probability (0.175 vs 0.174) and mask volume (80.0 mL) on the BHSD demo.

Self-test: node tools/hesigns_selftest.mjs injects each sign into a synthetic hematoma and checks it is found (and that a plain hematoma has none). On public demo cases the outputs were inspected by eye; no expert-read sign data set was available.
