Your images stay on this computer

Files are read and processed inside this browser tab on your GPU or CPU. No image data or results are uploaded. Only the page, the model files and the demo cases are downloaded, and the model is cached for later visits.

What you need

  • A recent desktop Chrome or Edge (Firefox and Safari may work on the CPU).
  • 8 GB RAM or more. WebGPU is fastest; on the CPU a scan takes one to several minutes.
  • A non-contrast axial head CT in Hounsfield units.

SAH Swin UNETR Río Hortega University Hospital, Valladolid · blood segmentation after aSAH

Blood on non-contrast head CT after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (one class: subarachnoid blood with any intraventricular or intraparenchymal extension), with volume. The segmentation stage of the Valladolid SAH mortality-prediction pipeline.

García-García, Cepeda et al. · Valladolid & Augsburg
Model files not installed. This copy of SAHVAI Web has no models/sahswin/ folder; convert the published checkpoint with tools/sahswin_export.py to enable this panel.
277 MB model download, cached after the first run. Runs on the CPU (WebAssembly) whatever the Compute setting: about 30 s per 128 × 128 × 64 window and usually two windows, so 1–2 minutes on a desktop; it needs about 2.3 GB of memory.
The network works in the authors’ 1 mm MNI CT template space. A scan from the RHUH dataset is already there and goes straight to the network; any other scan has its brain extracted (SAHVAI’s method; the original uses FSL BET) and is aligned to the template (rotation, shift and scale by mutual information; the original uses ANTs SyN), segmented, and mapped back so that volumes are measured on the scan itself.

Drop a head CT here

A zip of a DICOM study, a DICOM folder, loose DICOM files, a .nii / .nii.gz or a .nrrd volume. The RHUH demo cases are the dataset’s preprocessed, template-space scans; the BHSD cases are ordinary scans that are registered first.

The weights are converted to ONNX in full precision with no change (ONNX output matches PyTorch to 1.4 × 10⁻⁵, 0 voxels different). Two rewrites of the graph give the same arithmetic: the 2 × 2 × 2 transposed convolutions as 1 × 1 × 1 convolutions plus interleaving, and window attention computed in chunks of 64 windows so that it fits in the browser’s 4 GB memory. The 2 mm registration template is the repository’s 1 mm template, smoothed and subsampled. Its other model, the 3D DenseNet mortality predictor, is not distributed and not run here. CereBleed, the group’s newer multiclass tool (Cepeda S, et al. Neurosurgery 2026, doi:10.1227/neu.0000000000004020), is a separate model available at geibac.uva.es/cerebleed.

Demo data. RHUH cases 001 and 003 with their expert masks: Cepeda S, García-García S. Zenodo 2023, doi:10.5281/zenodo.8228847, CC BY 4.0. The model comes from the same hospital and these cases may have been used to develop it, so they show that the port is faithful, not how well the model generalises. BHSD cases as on the CLAIM page (labels: all blood).

How it runs here. As the repository’s inference_2.py (MONAI): Spacingd to 1.5 × 1.5 × 2 mm (bilinear) → z-score over the non-zero voxels → crop to the voxels above the mean → sliding window 128 × 128 × 64 with overlap 0.25 and constant blending → Swin UNETR (feature size 48) → logits back to the template (bilinear) → argmax. On RHUH cases 001–003 this port gives the same segmentation as the original Python pipeline (Dice 1.000; 63.0, 12.5 and 37.2 mL; Dice against the expert masks 0.77, 0.70 and 0.82). For other scans the original pipeline registers with ANTs SyN; in tests on three BHSD scans the original’s own result changed by Dice 0.79–0.93 between SyN and rigid registration, and this port agreed with its SyN result at Dice 0.77, 0.94 and 0.90. Volumes are measured on the scan; the original measures in template space, where they scale with head size.
